1. LICENCE AND PERMITTED USE

This software is a reporting tool that reads governance metrics from
a Pentaho Data Catalog instance over its public REST API and renders
them as dashboards, used in Pentaho training workshops and pre-sales
engagements. It is provided for use on authorised machines and by
authorised personnel only. You may install and use it for that
purpose. You may not sell, sublicense, rent or otherwise commercially
exploit the software, and you may not remove or alter any proprietary
notice it contains.


2. YOUR CATALOG

The software connects to the Pentaho Data Catalog instance you
configure and reads catalog metadata and aggregate counts (trust,
quality, sensitivity, coverage, lineage and similar measures). It is
READ-ONLY against the catalog: it exposes no operation that modifies
it. Everything it produces (dashboard specifications, resolved
values, explanations) is yours.

You are responsible for having the authority to connect to the
catalog with the account you configure, and for ensuring that doing
so does not breach an agreement, policy or regulation that applies to
it. Use a read-only, suitably scoped service account.

4. AI ASSISTANCE - NATURE, LIMITS AND OVERSIGHT

The software uses an artificial intelligence system to lay out
dashboard specifications from a plain-language request and to narrate
dashboard results. It runs against a local model (Ollama) or a hosted
provider you configure.

  (a) AI interaction. Content marked as AI-generated is produced by
      an AI system, not written by a person.

  (b) Outputs are probabilistic. Outputs are generated by a
      statistical language model and are inherently probabilistic.
      They may be factually incorrect, fabricated, out of date,
      incomplete, misleading or biased ("hallucinations"), may not
      reflect your catalog, and may differ between runs for the same
      input. Outputs are AI-generated content and should be treated
      as such.

  (c) No professional advice. Outputs are provided for informational
      purposes only and do not constitute professional advice of any
      kind, including legal, financial, regulatory or data protection
      advice.

  (d) Human oversight. A generated dashboard is presented for review
      before it is saved, and an explanation describes numbers you
      can see on screen; review both. You remain responsible for what
      you save, publish or act on.

  (e) The numbers are NOT decided by AI. Every value a dashboard
      panel shows is resolved deterministically from catalog reads
      (or the bundled sample); the language model only arranges
      panels and narrates results, and every generated specification
      is validated against a schema and the real query catalog before
      it can be saved. Do not modify the software to let AI supply
      the numbers.

  (f) Prompts and what is sent. Prompts include catalog metadata such
      as data-source names, query names and resolved aggregate values.
      Where you configure a HOSTED provider (Anthropic or OpenAI/
      Azure), that content leaves your machine and is processed under
      that provider's terms. Where you use a local model, it does
      not. Do not enable a hosted provider for metadata you may not
      disclose to a third party.

  (g) Output rights. No representation is made as to the ownership,
      originality or non-infringement of any Output. You are
      responsible for checking that your use of an Output does not
      infringe the rights of any third party. No indemnity is given
      in respect of Outputs.

  (h) Model components. Any AI model used is supplied by a third
      party under its own licence and use policy, which you are
      responsible for complying with. No warranty is given in respect
      of any such model.


5. DATA PROTECTION AND PRIVACY

  (a) Data kept on your machine. Settings (including the catalog
      connection), saved dashboards and the audit log are stored
      locally in the per-user data directory. The software transmits
      none of it except as described in (b) and (c).

  (b) Your catalog. The software only READS from the Pentaho Data
      Catalog instance you configure. It writes nothing to the
      catalog.

  (c) Hosted AI providers. See section 4(f). This is the only route
      by which catalog metadata can leave the machine, it is off
      unless you turn it on, and a local model avoids it entirely.

  (d) Credentials. The catalog service-account credentials are stored
      in the per-user data directory so that reads can be repeated.
      AI provider API keys are read from the environment and are
      deliberately NOT written to the settings file, so that
      exporting or sharing application state does not disclose them.
      Protect the per-user data directory accordingly.

  (e) Personal data in your catalog. Catalog metadata may include
      column names, business terms and sensitivity/PII findings that
      describe personal data held in your estate. The software reads
      such metadata as aggregate counts and asset names; enable a
      hosted AI provider only where you are entitled to disclose that
      metadata.

  (f) Controller. The organisation operating the software determines
      the purposes and means of this processing and is the data
      controller. It is responsible for its own privacy notice,
      lawful basis, retention periods, transfer mechanisms and
      processor terms, including in respect of any hosted AI provider
      it chooses to enable. The authors of the software supply a tool
      and operate no service on your behalf.

  (g) Your rights. Subject to applicable law you have the right to
      request access to, rectification of, or erasure of your
      personal data, to restrict or object to its processing, and to
      data portability, and to complain to your data protection
      supervisory authority. Exercise these rights with the
      organisation named in its privacy notice. Locally held data can
      be removed at any time by deleting the per-user data directory.

  (h) Data minimisation. Connect only the catalog you need, and
      prefer the local model where the narration features are used.
